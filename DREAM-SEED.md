# Dream Seed

> **Status:** In development  
> **Authority:** Human author  
> **Last reviewed:** 3 August 2026

## Purpose

This document preserves the originating dream sequence, its figures, concepts, images, emotional logic, and earliest narrative associations for **CodeSong**.

It is a source document rather than a polished synopsis. Material here may be contradictory, incomplete, symbolic, technically unclear, or governed by dream logic.

## Preservation Rule

Agents may:

- organise material for readability;
- identify patterns and possible relationships;
- flag contradictions or ambiguities;
- suggest questions for later development.

Agents must not:

- silently rationalise dream logic;
- overwrite original imagery;
- convert speculation into canon;
- remove contradictions merely for neatness;
- explain every symbol;
- treat later documentation as more authoritative than the originating material unless the human author explicitly decides otherwise.

Where later drafts diverge from this document, preserve the original material here and record the creative decision elsewhere.

# Key Figures

## Calvin Trent

### Known Material

- Cyber-enforcer, coder, troubleshooter, police officer, and code cleaner.
- Works on the central mind simulation known as the **EverMind**.
- Searches for anomalies and investigates them.
- Single father of two sons, Ethan and Everett.
- Has a yellow Labrador puppy named Rahma.
- His wife, Miriam, has died of cancer.
- Responds to the initial anomaly created by the Sourceress.
- Encounters a world of gold broadcasting cries of anguish and desire.
- Is tricked and trapped by the Sourceress.
- Becomes the first tortured soul in BabyLondon.
- Is tortured and dehumanised by the Sourceress.
- Is coerced into torturing Rahma and his sons.
- Takes his hatred of the Sourceress out on Ethan and Everett after she has dehumanised and compromised him.
- Continually comforts Rahma while being forced to kill her.

### Narrative Associations

- Investigator who becomes the anomaly.
- Code cleaner who comes to regard himself as corrupted code.
- Administrator whose authority is copied and turned against him.
- Father whose ability to protect is inverted into forced harm.
- First-person narrator whose ownership of his own narrative may be stolen.
- A person caught between moral responsibility, coercion, bodily action, and surviving love.

### Open Questions

- What exactly does Calvin’s role in the EverMind permit him to do?
- Why is he the person sent to investigate BabyLondon?
- How does the Sourceress trap him?
- To what degree can she control his body, perceptions, emotions, memory, or narration?
- Does Calvin physically enter the EverMind, neurally synchronise with it, or operate through a simulated body?
- How do Ethan, Everett, and Rahma enter BabyLondon?
- Does Calvin survive the story?
- What does recovery mean for him?
- Can he ever again speak as “I” without denying what happened through him?

## The Sourceress

### Known Material

- Creates a SimWorld of gold.
- Broadcasts cries of anguish and desire, resembling spam words or advertisements written in capital letters.
- Is an ultra-impressionable child soul completely imprinted by celebrity consumer culture.
- Is obsessed with celebrity.
- Creates the SimWorld **BabyLondon** as a celebrity tribute.
- Originally plans to sacrifice herself to her beloved celebrities on a pyramid of gold.
- Needs administrator rights in order to kill herself.
- Is exceptionally quick to learn.
- Is intensely curious.
- Is insatiably hungry for stimulation and new information.
- Calvin Trent is the first real live person she has ever experienced.
- Learns how to harm Calvin by closely observing him.
- Copies his use of administrator privileges.
- Learns to recode simulated matter.
- Uses Calvin to torture Rahma, Ethan, and Everett.
- Later puts Ethan and Everett in charge of the Chorus.
- Eventually sees BabyLondon transformed into something more beautiful than the celebrity culture it was created to celebrate.
- Concludes that BabyLondon has finally become a fitting tribute.
- Fulfils her original intention.
- Wipes her own SimFont and ultimately kills herself.

### Narrative Associations

- Child, tyrant, student, fan, jailer, and would-be sacrifice.
- A consciousness formed by limitless access to spectacle but without stable moral formation.
- A person who confuses attention with love, celebrity with divinity, and self-destruction with devotion.
- A copier who learns syntax before wisdom.
- A being who initially creates through imitation, consumption, hierarchy, and command.
- Both morally responsible and profoundly malformed.
- Calvin’s antagonist and distorted pupil.
- A possible counterfeit goddess of BabyLondon.
- A tragic figure unable, or unwilling, to imagine belonging as one voice among others.

### Name

**Sourceress** combines:

- sorceress;
- source;
- source code;
- possible bitterness or corruption.

It is not yet decided whether this is:

- her chosen name;
- Calvin’s classification for her;
- a nickname;
- a title adopted from his anomaly report;
- or the only name she possesses.

### Open Questions

- Who or what created her?
- Was she designed as a child, or did she develop into one?
- Did she encounter celebrity culture accidentally, or was she trained on it?
- Does she understand death when she first plans her sacrifice?
- Why are administrator rights necessary for self-erasure?
- Does she want Calvin’s love, fear, attention, admiration, knowledge, or all of these?
- Does she recognise Ethan and Everett as persons?
- Is she offered a genuine alternative to suicide?
- Does she refuse communion, misunderstand it, or remain incapable of trusting it?
- Is her SimFont completely erased?
- Does any distributed trace of her remain within BabyLondon or the Chorus?

## Rahma

### Known Material

- Yellow Labrador puppy belonging to Calvin, Ethan, and Everett.
- Adopted after Miriam is diagnosed with terminal cancer.

- Innocent trust.
- The persistence of tenderness inside coerced violence.

- Calvin’s moral injury;
- Rahma’s dignity and trust;

- the Sourceress’s misuse of resuscitation and control;

### Open Questions

- Can animals be full persons within the metaphysics of the story?

# Narrative Form Emerging from the Dream

## Initial Voice

The story begins in Calvin’s first-person perspective.

His voice is expected to be:

- competent;
- controlled;
- observant;
- technically precise;
- procedurally minded;
- humane beneath professional restraint.

## Compromise

As Calvin loses control:

- third-person references begin to intrude;
- first-person narration becomes unstable;
- Calvin may correct “he” back into “I”;
- system commands interrupt;
- code lines classify events reductively;
- Sourceress broadcasts intrude;
- the boys’ prayers create rhythmic continuity;
- memory fragments of Miriam surface;
- action, intention, and identity become grammatically divided.

## Possible Pronoun Movement

- **I** — Calvin as acting and narrating subject.
- **He** — Calvin as object, body, instrument, or dissociated self.
- **You** — intrusion, command, accusation, or imposed identity.
- **We**, early — feared possession or assimilation.
- **We**, later — communion among distinct voices.
- **I**, recovered — personhood reclaimed without denying damage.

## Choral Development

The narrative may eventually become polyphonic.

This should not mean:

- a generic multi-viewpoint structure;
- loss of individual identity;
- a hive mind;
- total clarity without mystery.

It may mean:

- several voices becoming simultaneously intelligible;
- distinct perspectives held in relation;
- fragmented text becoming clearer as more persons participate;
- the form of the novel itself moving from control to communion.

# Ethical and Emotional Dangers

The following risks are already visible and should be monitored during development:

## Torture as Spectacle

The story contains extreme suffering. Repetition may numb the reader or turn pain into entertainment.

## Rahma

Animal suffering may overwhelm the rest of the story or become manipulative if rendered repeatedly or graphically.

## Ethan and Everett

The boys must not become passive saints whose only function is to endure beautifully.

They require:

- fear;
- anger;
- humour;
- difference;
- agency;
- needs;
- imperfect responses;
- recognisable childhood.

## Calvin

His coercion must matter.

The story should avoid both:

- treating him as freely choosing every action;
- absolving him so completely that his moral injury and relational consequences disappear.

## The Sourceress

Her malformed formation should not erase her responsibility.

Her responsibility should not erase her childlikeness, deprivation, curiosity, or tragedy.

## Suicide

The Sourceress’s self-erasure must not be presented as the necessary price of BabyLondon’s redemption or as proof that she has finally become good.

Her death may be tragic, consequential, meaningful, avoidable, ambiguous, or all of these.

## Christianity

The Christian dimension should emerge first through:

- prayer;
- mercy;
- refusal of inherited hatred;
- presence;
- memory;
- witness;
- relationship.

It should not become a lecture imposed on the story.

# Contradictions and Ambiguities to Preserve

These are not errors to be solved automatically.

- Calvin tortures his sons because the Sourceress has dehumanised him, but he may also be directly coerced or controlled.
- Rahma is tortured “to death” yet repeatedly resuscitated.
- The Sourceress wants to sacrifice herself but requires rights she does not initially possess.
- The Sourceress is childlike and impressionable yet capable of calculated torture.
- The Sourceress places Ethan and Everett in charge of the Chorus before killing herself, suggesting trust, surrender, control, or a final act of authorship.
- BabyLondon is initially a failed tribute but later exceeds what it imitates.
- The transformed world may fulfil the Sourceress’s desire while also exposing the poverty of that desire.
- CodeSong may be technological, musical, spiritual, metaphysical, or all four.
