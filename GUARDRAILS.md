## Moral and Narrative Guardrails

1. The Sourceress’s suicide is tragic, not inherently redemptive.
2. The story must not imply that self-erasure is the proper answer to corruption, guilt or suffering.
3. Calvin’s coercion matters morally.
4. Calvin must not be treated as though he freely chose every action performed through his body.
5. Calvin’s moral injury must not be erased merely by establishing his technical innocence.
6. Forgiveness must not require denial of harm.
7. Ethan and Everett are not admirable because they suffer without protest.
8. The boys must remain recognisable children with fear, anger, humour, needs and distinct personalities.
9. Rahma’s suffering must not be used repeatedly for emotional escalation.
10. SimSouls are persons, not disposable metaphors for the development of RealSouls.
11. The Chorus must preserve individuality.
12. Communion must not become assimilation.
13. Beauty is not automatic evidence of goodness.
14. Administrator access is not moral authority.
15. Technical capability is not wisdom.
16. No person is merely corrupted code awaiting deletion.
17. The Sourceress must remain morally accountable without being reduced to a simple monster.
18. Her formation by celebrity consumer culture explains aspects of her condition but does not erase her agency.
19. Christian elements should emerge through character, practice and relationship rather than propaganda.
20. Hope must not require the story to pretend that irreversible damage never occurred.

## Process Guardrails

- Do not silently alter accepted canon.
- Do not overwrite human-authored prose without direct instruction.
- Present major rewrites as proposals or patches.
- Record settled decisions.
- Separate canonical material from speculation.
- Mark AI-generated additions clearly until accepted.
- Do not reopen locked decisions without new evidence or human instruction.
