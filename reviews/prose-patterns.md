# Prose-Patterns Review

> **Status:** In development  
> **Authority:** Human author  
> **Last reviewed:** 3 August 2026

## Scope

Accepted Chapters 1 and 2.

## Successful Calibration

The author accepted the opening’s mixture of funny, engaging and creepy. Its strongest recurring techniques are:

- procedural language used as character voice;
- dry institutional humour immediately followed by concrete threat;
- short code outputs that contradict or narrow human interpretation;
- repetition whose meaning changes, especially `THAT'S ME.` and `DID YOU LIKE IT?`;
- sensory details that make simulated space bodily without pausing for worldbuilding explanation;
- escalation through small permission discrepancies rather than spectacle.

## Patterns to Watch

- Aphoristic lines about machines, evidence or competence are effective but should remain sparse.
- Repeated constructions such as “I had not…” and “That was…” can create pressure, but overuse would make Calvin sound mechanically composed after compromise.
- Three-step procedural lists suit Calvin; later voices should not inherit the same cadence automatically.
- Capitalised broadcasts should remain selective enough to retain force.
- The Sourceress’s copied phrases should mutate through context rather than simply repeat.

## Development Direction

As Calvin fragments, reduce polished explanatory control while preserving local precision. As the Chorus emerges, clarity should return through relation among different cadences rather than through restoration of Calvin’s original monologue.
