# Trauma-and-Ethics Review

> **Status:** In development  
> **Authority:** Human author  
> **Last reviewed:** 3 August 2026

## Current Scope

Chapters 1 and 2 contain threat, surveillance, identity theft and bodily capture but do not yet depict torture, child suffering or animal suffering.

## Established Ethical Frame

- Calvin’s competence and care are visible before coercion.
- The narrative distinguishes his intention from the later use of his body without pretending enacted harm is unreal.
- The boys enter as practising children with humour and rules, not pre-formed symbols of innocent suffering.
- Rahma appears first as a loved, comic household presence.
- The Sourceress is treated as a person and childlike consciousness without excusing her choices.

## Required Review Before Family-Harm Scenes

- Concentrate Rahma’s suffering rather than repeating it for escalation.
- Give Ethan and Everett agency, anger, fear and imperfect responses.
- Preserve Calvin’s acts of comfort without using tenderness to sanitise violence.
- Keep the Sourceress’s curiosity from becoming an excuse that dissolves responsibility.
- Distinguish survival, revival, restoration and healing.
- Ensure the boys’ awakened faith does not make suffering appear instrumentally necessary.

## Ending Ethics

- The equal offer to Calvin and the Sourceress does not equate their histories or responsibilities.
- Calvin’s acceptance does not demand immediate forgiveness from his sons.
- The Sourceress’s refusal is not evidence that belonging was offered insincerely.
- Her final administrative usefulness is not repentance.
- Her loss of physical presence is not to be framed as a required cleansing of the community.
