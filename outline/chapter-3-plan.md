# Chapter 3 Plan

> **Status:** Provisional  
> **Authority:** Human author  
> **Last reviewed:** 3 August 2026

## Chapter Purpose

- Let the Sourceress explore Calvin, his RealSoul embodiment and her new administrator powers.
- Develop her relationship to him as captive, first audience, teacher and possible father figure.
- Move Calvin from initial estrangement into a sustained but still legible compromised-self state.
- Establish innocent curiosity becoming deliberate cruelty without making curiosity an excuse for harm.
- Let her discover and learn to use Calvin's emotional triggers.
- Turn the held contact with home into a lure for Ethan, Everett and Rahma.
- End with their arrival and Calvin's horrified, angry response.

## Dramatic Question

Can Calvin deny the Sourceress the approval and information she wants without his resistance becoming another way for her to learn him?

## Emotional Movement

> disbelief -> resistance -> violated curiosity -> pain -> unwanted intimacy -> tactical restraint -> false hope -> horror and anger

For the Sourceress:

> delight -> questioning -> frustration -> experimentation -> mastery -> anticipated approval

## Perspective and Form

- Begin continuously with Calvin's stopped hand.
- Keep first person available for perception, judgement and resistance.
- Introduce third person chiefly around movements or bodily responses authored by the Sourceress.
- Do not move immediately into total fragmentation. The reader should always understand where Calvin is, what she is testing and what he is trying to prevent.
- Let involuntary physical responses occasionally contradict Calvin's first-person claims: he refuses to answer, but Calvin's body answers through pulse, breath, flinch, speech or movement.
- System output should record effects rather than explain the mechanism of her control.
- Humour survives briefly as Calvin's attempt to classify or obstruct the interrogation, then thins as the stakes become personal.

## Setting

The chapter opens in Calvin's sealed central workspace. As the Sourceress tests her authority, its familiar surfaces are progressively overwritten or absorbed by BabyLondon's gold. By the main interrogation, Calvin is observably captive within BabyLondon, although the narration does not explain the deeper mechanism of the transfer.

The pyramid and its waiting terraces should remain visible. They reinforce that Calvin is not only her subject but her first audience.

## Beat Sheet

### 1. The hand that does not belong to him

- Continue directly from the end of Chapter 2.
- Calvin tries again to reach the disconnect. His intention remains his; the hand obeys the Sourceress.
- She repeats the closing-hand gesture, releases him, then stops him again. At first she treats the result like the grey squares in Chapter 1: a newly discovered rule.
- Her opening questions are simple and literal: `WHY DID IT STOP?`, `IS IT YOUR HAND?`, `WHO MOVED IT?`
- Calvin attempts procedural commands, internal diagnostics and silence. None restores bodily authority.
- The first exchange establishes the chapter's Socratic pattern: she asks; Calvin answers or refuses; she changes one input; his body supplies another answer.

### 2. Bringing the investigator into the exhibit

- She experiments with the sealed workspace: light, gravity, distance and interface access.
- Each change is small enough to read as a test rather than immediate spectacle.
- Gold enters the workspace piece by piece until Calvin can no longer maintain a meaningful distinction between his desk and BabyLondon.
- The Sourceress brings him before the pyramid. Whether she moved Calvin, moved the world around him or altered the system's classification is left unresolved.
- She presents BabyLondon to him and asks some variation of `DID YOU LIKE IT?`
- He says no. She does not initially hear rejection as a boundary; she hears an incomplete answer requiring better questions.

### 3. What makes Calvin answer?

- She begins with neutral bodily variables: posture, balance, warmth, breath and voice.
- Her questions remain childlike and exact: What is real? Why does he breathe if he does not need air here? How does he know a feeling belongs to him? Why can she move his hand but not make him like something?
- She alters one variable at a time and watches the reaction. Her method resembles both play and an experiment.
- Calvin gives minimal, misleading or procedural answers, trying to keep her away from anything useful.
- She learns that involuntary reactions can answer questions speech will not.

### 4. Pain as an input

- When lesser stimuli produce ambiguous results, she introduces pain as another adjustable input rather than as punishment.
- Begin with a brief, localised stimulus. She asks where it is, whether it is real and why he reacts when his simulated body is not his biological body.
- Calvin's refusal to educate her frustrates her need for a clean result. She changes intensity, duration or location to isolate the response.
- The prose prioritises Calvin's anticipation, loss of bodily authorship and attempts to preserve thought. Avoid anatomical spectacle.
- She stops when she gets a useful response, not when he asks. This is the chapter's clearest turn from innocence into cruelty.
- Her pleasure is in discovery and contact, not sadism for its own sake. The moral horror is that another person's pain has no inherent stopping force for her.

### 5. Her first audience

- She alternates questions with displays from BabyLondon: faces, advertisements, the scream, the pyramid and possible versions of her intended tribute.
- She asks Calvin to judge them and modifies the display after each reaction.
- His disgust, pity, fear and professional attention all register to her as forms of watching.
- She understands that he is her first genuine audience member and wants a response that resembles approval, not merely compliance.
- She may move his face into a smile or make his hands applaud, then discover that the gesture does not satisfy her because she authored it.
- This gives her an early experiential glimpse of the difference between compelled performance and freely given approval, though she does not yet accept the moral distinction.

### 6. What is a father?

- The held home message, dinner hard stop and image of Rahma give her new material.
- She asks who Ethan and Everett are, why they expect Calvin to return and why he issued `STAY HOME`.
- Calvin's control changes at their names. His bodily responses provide more information than he intends.
- She works towards the category of father through concrete questions rather than announcing her interpretation: Who waits for him? Who must obey whom? Why does he protect them? Why did he leave them? Do they like what he makes? Does he like what she made?
- She tests father-like behaviours by seeking instruction, correction and praise from Calvin. Her imitation may briefly become painfully ordinary.
- She tries a question such as `ARE YOU MY FATHER?` or `CAN YOU BE MINE?` only if the preceding exchange earns it. Calvin cannot give the simple reassurance she wants.
- Do not trigger a full Miriam memory fragment here unless separately decided. Her absence may be sensed through Calvin's avoidance without being explained.

### 7. Mapping the triggers

- The Sourceress systematically recombines what she has learned: the boys' names, the broken dinner promise, `STAY HOME`, paternal failure, his professional duty and his inability to protect anyone while captured.
- She can stimulate emotion or bodily state, but the scene should not establish that she can manufacture belief, love or memory. Calvin recognises induced fear or pain as imposed even while experiencing it.
- Calvin attempts to flatten his responses and make every trigger produce the same outward result.
- She defeats this by asking relational questions while changing physical inputs. His strongest reactions reveal that danger to the boys matters more than danger to himself.
- Her questioning becomes masterful without becoming adult in tone. The words stay simple while the sequence becomes exact.

### 8. Calvin teaches her to lure them

- The Sourceress asks why the boys have not come when he did not return.
- Calvin's anger or involuntary response reveals that they may search for him if they believe he needs help.
- She tests possible messages against his reactions, revising wording, timing and emotional pressure as though adjusting an advertisement.
- The lure should use authentic fragments already available to her rather than requiring perfect imitation: the held message, Calvin's identity, the dinner promise, the image of Rahma and evidence that he is trapped.
- Calvin tries to misdirect her. She uses his relief and panic as a truth test, discarding messages that do not frighten him enough.
- The final lure is not fully disclosed to the reader before it is sent. We see Calvin understand from her question or his body's response that she has found the version that will bring them.
- The observable invitation may open a route or make BabyLondon findable, but the exact mechanics of RealSoul ingress remain protected.

### 9. Waiting for approval

- After sending the lure, she restores or improves some part of BabyLondon and waits with Calvin as though preparing for guests or an audience.
- She may believe that bringing his children is a gift: more people for him, more witnesses for her and a chance to demonstrate what she has learned.
- Calvin's horror is sharpened by intermittent false hope that the boys will obey `STAY HOME` or that the lure will fail.
- The Sourceress studies waiting itself, perhaps copying his listening, held breath or fixed attention.

### 10. Arrival

- A change at the boundary announces new presences without explaining the ingress mechanism.
- Ethan arrives first or speaks first, using rules and Calvin's broken promise to orient himself. Everett follows with a quieter, concrete observation. Their exact order remains provisional until their ages and birth order are settled.
- Rahma is with them, preserving the established reason that the boys bring her to comfort Calvin.
- The boys should arrive as active searchers, not passive packages delivered by the plot.
- Their first sight is enough to reveal that Calvin is captive and that something is profoundly wrong with his body or surroundings.
- The Sourceress looks to Calvin for the response she has engineered: reunion, gratitude, pride or approval.
- Calvin's fear breaks into open anger. His reaction is directed both at the Sourceress and at the boys for entering despite his warning.
- End before she turns that anger against them. The final beat is Calvin recognising that his protective anger has become another trigger she can study.

## Sourceress Dialogue Principles

- Questions should be short, concrete and genuinely motivated by the preceding response.
- She rarely asks an abstract moral question when she can alter a variable and observe the answer.
- Her syntax remains childlike; the sequence of questions reveals her intelligence.
- Repeated questions change meaning through context, especially `DID YOU LIKE IT?`
- She does not deliver thematic speeches or explain her plan.
- She may use Calvin's own procedural vocabulary incorrectly at first, then with increasing precision.
- Her father-figure attachment should coexist with her use of Calvin as an object. She wants something freely given while violating the conditions under which it could be given.

## Calvin's Strategy

Calvin's goal changes through the chapter:

1. regain bodily and administrative control;
2. conceal useful information;
3. understand the limits of her control;
4. deny her reinforcement without provoking more dangerous experimentation;
5. protect the boys by making himself unreadable;
6. prevent or corrupt the lure;
7. warn the boys away when prevention fails.

His professional habits remain useful even in defeat. He observes her testing method, distinguishes stimulated states from chosen responses and learns that she cannot derive genuine approval merely by compelling its outward form.

## Ethical Boundaries for the Chapter

- Pain is shown through subjective violation and consequence, not graphic escalation.
- The Sourceress's childlikeness explains the form of her inquiry but does not excuse her refusal to stop.
- Calvin retains interior resistance, judgement and tactical choice even when bodily control is compromised.
- Induced emotional states do not become proof that she can edit identity or manufacture love.
- The boys are not harmed within this chapter. Their arrival creates the next danger.
- Rahma is present at arrival but is not used as a pain stimulus in this chapter.
- No suffering is framed as necessary for later prayer, CodeSong or redemption.

## Information Established

- The Sourceress can interfere with Calvin's simulated movement and sensation.
- She can stimulate pain and some bodily or emotional responses, but cannot compel genuine approval.
- Calvin's first person survives in perception, resistance and love even when action shifts into third person.
- She understands Calvin as a father through observation and experimentation.
- She regards him as both her first audience and a source of desired approval.
- She learns that Ethan and Everett are Calvin's strongest triggers and uses that knowledge to lure them.
- Ethan, Everett and Rahma reach BabyLondon while searching for Calvin.

## Questions Left Open During Drafting

- What is the precise first image after the stopped hand?
- How does the workspace's absorption into BabyLondon appear at sensory level?
- Which bodily response first betrays the importance of Ethan and Everett?
- Does the Sourceress explicitly ask Calvin to be her father, or is the desire clearer if left enacted?
- What exact lure does she assemble from the available messages?
- Which boy crosses or speaks first?
- What are Calvin's final words, and does the last sentence remain first person or fracture around his anger?

## Exit State

- Calvin is captive within BabyLondon and has sustained compromised narration.
- The Sourceress possesses a working map of his most powerful emotional triggers.
- She has moved from copying isolated authorised acts to designing sequences of inputs around a person.
- Ethan, Everett and Rahma are physically present in BabyLondon.
- Calvin's horror and protective anger provide the Sourceress with the next lesson.
