# Part Two: The House of Gold

> **Status:** Provisional  
> **Authority:** Human author  
> **Last reviewed:** 4 August 2026

The title and allocation of later events remain provisional. The opening boundary is accepted: Chapter 4 begins Part Two with the consequences of the family’s arrival. The outcomes below are directional canon unless explicitly labelled proposed; their scene form is not fixed.

## Calvin’s Compromise

Chapter 3 has already established Calvin’s compromised grammar and the Sourceress’s initial bodily controls. The later movement may deepen that division through third person, code, commands, broadcasts, prayer and first-person fragments without losing the basic event sequence.

## Expansion of BabyLondon

The Sourceress lures people into BabyLondon to witness her tributes and to satisfy her curiosity. The world moves from one registered resident towards a culture capable of spectacle, suffering and eventually genuine creation.

The family arrives before any wider recruited population appears in accepted prose. The identities, Soul categories and precise arrival sequence of the later people remain open.

## Arrival of the Family

Accepted Chapter 3 establishes that Ethan and Everett come looking for Calvin after he fails to return and bring Rahma to comfort him. They arrive with a route record and lead, identify Calvin’s compromised smile and refuse to leave him. Calvin is surprised, appalled and angry that they have exposed themselves to BabyLondon; the Sourceress begins studying that fear and anger.

This arrival closes Part One. Part Two begins with the relational and bodily consequences rather than replaying the entrance from a separate movement.

The exact mechanism of RealSoul ingress remains protected.

## Coercion

Calvin is tortured and dehumanised, then forced to harm Rahma and his sons. He continues to comfort and love them. His coercion, the reality of the harm and the relational consequences must all remain present.

Rahma’s suffering should be implied and concentrated rather than repeatedly rendered for escalation.

## The Boys’ Resistance

Ethan and Everett remain distinct children with fear, anger, humour and needs. Their love does not deny the harm or make them passive saints.

- Ethan tends towards structure, verbal clarity, protection and rules.
- Everett tends towards play, intuition, musical receptivity and quiet humour.

## Miriam’s Remembered Presence

Miriam does not enter the opening work chapters as conscious exposition. She returns through Calvin’s fractured memory, the boys’ practice, Rahma and family gestures.

## Prayer

Miriam’s Eastern Catholic faith has already formed the boys. Their inherited root prayer is: “Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me.” They enter BabyLondon practising Christians. Suffering and tragic loyalty to Calvin awaken their inherited practice into deliberate prayer, resistance and witness.

Prayer acts upon people and the EverMind before anyone offers a theological explanation.

Calvin later becomes the first to add “a sinner” as his own freely chosen confession. Ethan, Everett and any other voices need not adopt his addition.

## The Harmonious Triad

Ethan and Everett become two members of the Harmonious Triad. The Sourceress reconstructs Mira, a third conscious SimSoul, from Calvin and the boys’ archived memories of Miriam, intending her as tribute, experiment and coercive gift. Calvin rejects Mira as a simulacrum because she is not his wife. She knows that she is a reconstruction, chooses her own name and becomes a genuine source of beauty by contributing something absent from every archive.

This configuration is accepted directional canon. Mira is a new SimSoul rather than Miriam resurrected. Her exact creation, memory structure, first original contribution and renaming scene remain open. Rahma remains the non-verbal family dog and is not counted as the third member.

## Discovery of Music

Their assigned tribute becomes genuine music and beauty, initiating the Symphony and a cultural explosion. The causal bridge from propaganda to music must be dramatised rather than explained in advance.
