# Calvin Trent

> **Status:** In development  
> **Authority:** Human author  
> **Last reviewed:** 4 August 2026

## Role in the Story

EverMind cyber-enforcer and anomaly investigator; widowed father of Ethan and Everett; initial first-person narrator; the first person captured and tortured in BabyLondon.

## Known History

- Works as a coder, troubleshooter, police officer and code cleaner within the EverMind.
- Uses spoken code and thought-code with high competence.
- Was married to Miriam Trent, who died of cancer.
- Quietly blames himself for her death and wishes he had died instead.
- Does not share Miriam’s Eastern Catholic faith.
- Continues taking Ethan and Everett to church to honour her.
- Already regards himself as an inadequate father before BabyLondon.
- Responds to the anomalous BabyLondon broadcast and becomes the first RealSoul the Sourceress encounters.
- Is named as the source of the administrator authority she copies.
- Is captured after she revokes his access and seals his workspace.
- Becomes the first tortured soul in BabyLondon when the Sourceress manipulates his simulated body and uses pain to obtain involuntary answers.
- Remains internally capable of judgement, refusal and witness while she compels some movement, expression and speech.
- Refuses to praise BabyLondon, become the Sourceress’s father or treat need as consent.
- Inadvertently teaches her how to lure Ethan, Everett and Rahma by reacting to candidate messages.
- Warns the family to leave when they arrive, then turns protective fear into his own anger at the boys’ disobedience.
- Is later coerced into harming Rahma, Ethan and Everett.
- Eventually accepts the Chorus’s offer and becomes the first character to add “a sinner” to the Jesus Prayer as a freely chosen personal confession.

## Personality

- Cynical in the recognisable register of a cop investigator.
- Dryly humorous, especially around procedure and institutional absurdity.
- Highly intelligent, perceptive and evidentially minded.
- Notices small procedural inconsistencies and checks obvious facts because records matter.
- Competent enough that his error must arise from an unprecedented threat model, not carelessness.
- Quietly compassionate beneath restraint.
- Pushes grief behind work rather than narrating it openly.

## Desires

### Canonical

- To protect Ethan, Everett and Rahma.
- To honour Miriam through care for the family even without sharing her belief.
- To identify, contain and document threats in the EverMind.

### Working inference

- To prove to himself that competence can compensate for the loss he could not prevent.

## Fears

### Canonical

- Failing his sons as a father.
- That he should have died instead of Miriam.
- Later, that his body and authority will be used against those he loves.

## Relationships

- **Miriam:** late wife, source of buried grief, guilt and continuing family practice.
- **Ethan:** son whose concern with rules meets Calvin’s procedural mind and broken promises.
- **Everett:** son whose playfulness and quiet humour reach Calvin through his professional shell.
- **Rahma:** dependent, comic home presence and living bond with Miriam.
- **The Sourceress:** first investigated subject, distorted pupil, captor and person offered the same possible belonging.
- **The Chorus:** community whose offer he eventually accepts.

## Character Arc

Competent acting subject → administrator whose authority is copied → captive estranged from his body and grammar → coerced father who continues to love while causing harm → person unable to repair himself by command → recipient of an equal offer → freely confessing participant who heals with time in communion.

## Narrative Function

Calvin’s shifting grammatical person makes bodily authorship, moral injury and recovery formal properties of the story.

## Symbolic Associations

- Investigator who becomes the anomaly.
- Code cleaner who may come to regard himself as corrupted code.
- Father whose protective authority is inverted into harm.
- The `I` that survives in witness, love and eventual participation.

These associations are not exhaustive interpretations.

## Voice Notes

- Controlled first person at the opening.
- Procedural explanation only where Calvin would naturally use it.
- Understated jokes rather than performed cleverness.
- Miriam absent from his conscious opening narration.
- Third person first intrudes around his stopped hand at the end of Chapter 2.
- In Chapter 3, first person carries intention, judgement and witness while `Calvin`, `he` and `his` chiefly mark externally authored bodily action.
- Dry procedure survives as resistance during interrogation, but polished control thins as pain and family danger intensify.

## Canonical Facts

- Calvin is a RealSoul.
- Chapters 1–3 establish his initial voice, capture, first torture, compromised grammar and reaction to the family’s arrival.
- He remains capable of love and comfort while being coerced into harm.
- He eventually breaks down and accepts belonging, healing with time, contribution and communion.
- His addition “a sinner” is voluntary, personal and not part of the form Miriam taught the boys.

## Speculative Possibilities

- Calvin’s professional competence may partly function as a defence against grief and paternal inadequacy. This is a working inference, not yet explicit canon.
