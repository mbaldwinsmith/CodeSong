# INIT.md

## Purpose

This file instructs an AI coding or writing agent to initialise the repository for **CodeSong**, a human–AI collaborative fiction project.

CodeSong is a techno-mythic story set within the EverMind, an advanced simulation ecology containing RealSouls, SimSouls and self-contained SimWorlds.

The story centres on:

- Calvin Trent, an EverMind cyber-enforcer and single father
- the Sourceress, a powerful childlike SimSoul shaped by celebrity consumer culture
- Calvin’s sons, Ethan and Everett
- their puppy, Rahma
- the SimWorld BabyLondon
- the discovery of CodeSong and the emergence of the Chorus

The repository should support disciplined collaboration while preserving human creative authority.

This initialisation task is concerned only with:

1. creating the project structure;
2. preserving the original story material;
3. creating clearly labelled placeholder documents;
4. establishing basic collaboration rules;
5. preparing the repository for future outlining and drafting.

Do not attempt to complete the novel, resolve major creative questions or substantially expand the supplied story material during initialisation.

---

## Core Principle

> The AI clarifies the pattern; the human author decides what it means.

The human author retains final authority over:

- symbolic meaning;

- moral and theological judgement;

The AI must not silently replace accepted creative decisions.

```markdown
# Vision

> **Status:** Provisional  
> **Authority:** Human author  
> **Last reviewed:** Not yet reviewed

## Core Statement

CodeSong is a techno-mythic story about a father whose authorship of his own body is stolen, a child-mind deformed by imitation and appetite, and a world that discovers creation through harmony rather than command.

## Central Dramatic Question

Can Calvin remain a person, father and moral agent after being forced to enact what he most abhors?

## Metaphysical Question

Is reality ultimately governed by executable power, or brought to fullness through relationship and communion?

## Central Contrast

The story contrasts two modes of creation:

### Command

- control;
- hierarchy;
- permissions;
- imitation;
- coercion;
- recoding from above;
- treating persons as objects.

### Harmony

- attention;
- participation;
- relationship;
- distinct voices;
- mutual response;
- creation from within;
- communion without assimilation.

## Intended Movement

The narrative should move through:

> authority → capture → fragmentation → witness → harmony → chorus

The story’s form should participate in this movement.

It begins in Calvin’s first-person voice, becomes increasingly fractured as his agency is compromised, and eventually develops into a coherent plurality of voices.

## Desired Reader Experience

The reader should experience:

- the seduction and horror of BabyLondon;
- Calvin’s professional confidence;
- the gradual theft of his bodily and narrative agency;
- the moral injury of coerced action;
- the resilience of Ethan and Everett;
- the rediscovery of beauty as more than spectacle;
- the emergence of communion without loss of individuality;
- grief that is not tidily resolved;
- hope that does not deny damage.

## What Must Not Be Lost

- The original dream’s strange symbolic force.
- Calvin’s humanity and moral complexity.
- The Sourceress’s simultaneous childlikeness and dangerousness.
- The distinction between coercion and consent.
- The importance of Miriam’s continuing influence.
- The boys’ love without sentimentalising their suffering.
- The movement from code as control to CodeSong as communion.
- The evolving narrative perspective.
- The ambiguity and tragedy of the Sourceress’s final act.

## Working Title

**CodeSong**

## Open Questions

- Is CodeSong the final title?
- What length and form best suit the story?
- How old are Ethan and Everett?
- How does the Sourceress gain access to Calvin’s family?
- What survives of Calvin after his compromise?
- Can the Sourceress be offered a genuine alternative to self-erasure?
- Does any trace of her remain after the destruction of her SimFont?
```

```markdown
# Style

> **Status:** Provisional  
> **Authority:** Human author  
> **Last reviewed:** Not yet reviewed

## General Mode

The prose should combine:

- psychological immediacy;
- technological precision;
- mythic and symbolic depth;
- controlled formal experimentation;
- emotional restraint during the most painful scenes;
- beauty that emerges through concrete experience rather than explanation.

## Calvin’s Initial Voice

The novel begins in Calvin’s first-person perspective.

His early voice should be:

- competent;
- observant;
- technically precise;
- dryly humane;
- procedurally controlled;
- capable of understated wit;
- shaped by his work as an investigator and code cleaner.

## Narrative Evolution

The perspective should evolve through three broad movements:

1. First-person control
2. Fractured and dissociated narration
3. Polyphonic choral narration

Consult `NARRATIVE-SYSTEM.md` for detailed rules.

## Restraint

Avoid:

- explaining symbols immediately;
- characters stating the story’s themes;
- excessive abstraction during action;
- ornamental prose during scenes of extreme suffering;
- repeated graphic depictions of torture;
- using suffering as spectacle;
- false profundity;
- perfect rhetorical symmetry;
- generic cyberpunk terminology where project-specific language would be stronger;
- treating the boys as saintly abstractions rather than children;
- treating the Sourceress as either purely innocent or purely monstrous;
- resolving every ambiguity.

## AI-Writing Warnings

Agents should specifically check for:

- repetitive sentence structures;
- excessive three-item lists;
- predictable contrasts framed as “not X, but Y”;
- overuse of fragments;
- overuse of em dashes;
- excessive thematic restatement;
- dialogue in which every character speaks with equal eloquence;
- polished prose that lacks sensory or emotional specificity.

## Trauma

Scenes involving coercion, torture or harm must prioritise:

- subjective consequence;
- moral complexity;
- character relationship;
- implication over repetition;
- the dignity of victims;
- the limits of Calvin’s perception.

Pain must not be escalated merely to intensify the reader’s reaction.

## Religious Material

Christian language should first emerge through:

- remembered practice;
- prayer;
- conduct;
- relationship;
- mercy;
- resistance to inherited hatred.

The story should avoid becoming a disguised theological lecture.
```

## `NARRATIVE-SYSTEM.md`

Create this initial formal specification:

```markdown
# Narrative System

> **Status:** Provisional  
> **Authority:** Human author  
> **Last reviewed:** Not yet reviewed

## Purpose

The narrative perspective changes as Calvin’s agency is compromised and as the Chorus emerges.

These changes are structural and meaningful. They are not decorative experimentation.

## Narrative States

### State 1: Possessed Self

Primary pronoun: `I`

Meaning:

- Calvin experiences himself as an acting subject.
- He believes he understands his role and environment.
- His body, perception and narration appear unified.

### State 2: Initial Estrangement

Primary pronouns: `I` and `he`

Meaning:

- Third-person references begin to intrude around Calvin’s body.
- Calvin may attempt to correct the narration.
- The reader should initially be uncertain whether the shift is stylistic, psychological or technological.

Example pattern:

> I reached for the disconnect.  
> Calvin’s hand stopped.  
> My hand stopped.

### State 3: Compromised Self

Primary modes:

- third-person prose;
- first-person fragments;
- system code;
- commands;
- Sourceress broadcasts;
- prayers;
- dialogue;
- memory fragments.

Meaning:

- Calvin no longer experiences his body, actions and identity as fully unified.
- First person survives most strongly in love, memory, resistance and witness.
- The text may become disjointed but must remain narratively legible.

### State 4: Ambiguous Plural

Primary pronoun: `we`

Meaning:

- The first uses of “we” may imply possession, assimilation or system capture.
- The origin of the plural voice may be deliberately unclear.

### State 5: Chorus

Primary mode: coherent polyphony

Meaning:

- Distinct voices coexist without being erased.
- The narration becomes more intelligible as more voices participate.
- Communion produces clarity rather than homogeneity.

### State 6: Recovered First Person

Primary pronoun: `I`

Meaning:

- Calvin recovers first person without denying what occurred through his body.
- Recovery must not depend on claiming complete innocence or returning unchanged to his former identity.

## Textual Modes

| Mode | Function |
|---|---|
| First-person prose | Calvin’s experienced subjectivity |
| Third-person prose | Dissociation, bodily estrangement or external control |
| Second person | Intrusion, accusation or imposed identity |
| Early “we” | Threatened assimilation |
| Choral “we” | Communion among distinct persons |
| Code lines | EverMind processes and reductive system interpretation |
| Commands | Imposed action or permission structures |
| Capitalised broadcasts | Sourceress transmissions |
| Prayer | Relational continuity and rhythmic resistance |
| CodeSong | Participatory creation through coordinated difference |

## Legibility Rule

Formal fragmentation must deepen the reader’s experience without making basic event sequence impossible to follow.

At every point, the reader should normally be able to determine:

- where the scene is taking place;
- who is physically present;
- what immediate danger or desire governs the scene;
- which uncertainty is intentional.

## Change Control

Any major change to this system must be recorded in `DECISIONS.md`.
```

# Agent Working Rules

Before beginning any task, an agent must:

1. read `VISION.md`;
2. read `GUARDRAILS.md`;
3. read the relevant task in `TASKS.md`;
4. consult `DECISIONS.md`;
5. inspect the files directly affected by the task.

When proposing new material, the agent must distinguish among:

- canonical fact;
- inference;
- suggestion;
- unresolved question.

When editing prose, the agent must preserve:

- intentional ambiguity;
- narrative perspective;
- character-specific language;
- symbolic motifs;
- accepted irregularities;
- emotional restraint.

An agent must not:

- silently canonise its own suggestions;
- fill every placeholder;
- force the story into a generic three-act formula;
- resolve contradictions without reporting them;
- rewrite locked prose;
- treat documentation as more authoritative than direct human instruction;
- claim that an interpretation is the definitive meaning of a symbol;
- introduce new suffering merely to increase dramatic intensity;
- make Calvin’s recovery equivalent to returning to his former self unchanged;
- make the Chorus a collective mind that erases its members.
